My Testimony

Why I Believe in Christ

A child's questions

As a child, I heard about God, but I wondered who He really was, what He felt, and why we are here. I thought: if God created us, why wouldn't He leave a message? I understood that message was the Bible. But when I was young, I only understood that Christ came and died for our sins, and that was all. It didn't answer my questions. I thought, "Thank you, Christ, but I don't know what to do with that."

Living in darkness

Without a purpose, I lived like an animal. I took what I wanted and did whatever I wanted. I knew there were consequences, but I didn't believe sin could hurt God, because He is all powerful. Still, every sin left me empty and alone. Hell is the absence of God, and in a way I was already tasting it. But God was not completely absent, because I felt repentance. For years I prayed, asking forgiveness and asking Him to lead me the right way, even though I didn't know who I was talking to. I carried the burden of feeling alone, sinful and hypocritical, like an animal hunting the same prey every day.

As I matured, I saw the other side: how much damage my actions could cause to others. I concluded that I was alone in this world and no one could have my back. Today I believe something different: we are alone only when we are without God. I decided to stop hurting people and not be a stone in the road for others. That path led me to meet my wife.

Questions, honesty and a decision

She had recently become a Christian and had strong faith. I had many questions, and I am not a good reader, so she helped me with answers. I liked the God she believed in, but I stayed skeptical. I didn't want to believe just because she did.

After five years together, we had to decide: marriage or breaking up. I asked if she would be willing to go to church by herself. She was heartbroken. But in that moment we both became humble. My heart was open to listen, and she said, "I don't have all the answers, but let's find them together." We agreed I would go to church once or twice a month, and that we would teach our children faith, so they would have something to hold on to. That night I knew I wanted to marry her. I also challenged myself: did Jesus really die on the cross, was He God, and did He fulfill the promises?

Then I proposed, and we started premarital classes. I brought all my questions there. The couple guiding us was skeptical of me, and that was fair.

I started with logic. I compared atheism, polytheism and monotheism, and monotheism made the most sense. I compared the main monotheistic religions and saw that Christianity, Judaism and Islam share one root, divided over time, a pattern the Bible itself describes. Christianity made the most sense to me, but I still didn't believe in Christ. The premarital couple asked me to read the Gospel of John.

Two months alone in Europe

Before marriage, I traveled alone to Europe for two months to learn to be independent, working remotely. I enjoyed great places and meals, but I realized none of it mattered if I couldn't share it.

John begins with the light shining in the darkness. That was me: full of darkness I kept sealed so I wouldn't hurt others. The light is Jesus Christ. John the Baptist came to testify about the light, and I found that purpose beautiful. In John 1:10-11 I understood that Jesus existed before coming to the world. He is not just a man using God's power; He is God, the Creator. The message I wanted as a child was Jesus Himself.

Like Scrooge's ghosts, God put people in my path. A close friend gave me company, yet I still felt alone. My German Airbnb host, who had lost his fiancée and his job just before marriage, was a warning to me, and when I felt alone he told me, "God is with you." When I met my friend again, I no longer felt alone, and now I was the one asking him: why not one God?

I also saw temptation, and the beauty that covers sin and makes it hard to recognize. I resisted some temptations and failed others. When I failed, I felt naked, as if God was looking deeply at me. I got sick and feared I could die alone, and I avoided the doctor. Sin is like that sickness: dangerous, but we keep going in survival mode, alone.

At the Castello Aragonese in Ischia, I met an opera singer in her seventies or eighties, traveling alone. She sang opera in the empty church, only for God. Later she told me she had lost someone with my name who looked like me. She hugged me, cried and spoke in German, maybe to her loved one. I cried with her. Today I understand Matthew 6: God sees in secret. She was worshipping Him in secret.

Around that time I met another man who had just become a father. He spoke openly and proudly about his sexual life, and he told me to enjoy as much as I could before marriage, because after it I would not be able to taste "many different flavors." I think he was trying to be kind to me, and I still consider him a friend. But I believe he was wrong. Behind his pride I felt emptiness, and I saw what sin looks like: a man who did not know God, alone in his life, who felt he had already lived the best of it.

While I was reading John, I visited the Vatican and saw cloth said to belong to John the Baptist and to Simon Peter. Standing in front of it, the people I was reading about felt real and historical to me.

In A Coruña I saw hundreds of pilgrims of the Camino de Santiago arriving together, united as a brotherhood. I thought: this is how arriving in heaven will feel. Many people walking the way, becoming the body of Christ, and ending in the same place to contemplate the beauty.

Accompanied

I kept reading the Bible. And for the first time in my life, even while I was alone, I felt completely accompanied, not only by people who love me, but by Someone greater. The loneliness that followed me since childhood found its answer in God, through His word and through Jesus Christ, the light that shines in the darkness.

Coming back

When I returned, I told the premarital couple that I believed Jesus Christ is my Lord and Savior, because He had accompanied me on the trip like a familiar loving person I had not met yet. I was honest that I still had questions, and that I was afraid I could be derailed from this truth. They were moved, and they approved our marriage. In the remaining meetings, I brought every question I had from John: why Jesus behaved the way He did, what "the Word" means, and much more.

Believing in God as a Christian brought some judgment from family and friends. But I knew that finding God was more important than the risk of losing them. With time, those relationships got better.

A purpose I didn't know I had

Ephesians 5 struck me deeply: husbands are called to love their wives as Christ loved the church. It is a mission, and it gave me a purpose I didn't know I had. The child who asked "why are we here?" found an answer.

In our first year and eight months of marriage, we have had so much fun, learned from each other, and tried to make our relationship with God deeper and more serious. We took courses and tried to get closer to Him every day.

In those courses, I studied who Jesus is and what He came to do. I understood that He is 100% God and 100% human, and that He carried our sins and God's anger for all that sin. Our sin leads to death (Romans 6:23). In the Old Testament, a pure lamb was sacrificed for the sins of the people, and Jesus is that Lamb, the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world (John 1:29). He died in my place, and by His grace I am forgiven. But He also came to defeat our biggest enemy, death (1 Corinthians 15:26). He rose from the dead, still 100% God and 100% human.

In this time, Genesis 6:5-6 impacted me: God saw the wickedness of man, and it grieved Him in His heart. I remembered how, in my years of darkness, I believed my sin could not hurt God because He is all powerful. Now I know it did grieve Him, not because He is weak, but because He loves me.

The prayer and the storm

Things were going so well that we became afraid. We prayed: "God, we are scared of your will. We know things have been good. Show us your will, bring us closer to you and to community, and show us the way."

One week later, we had to leave our apartment because it was sold, I lost my job in a large layoff, and I had health issues to check. God showed us our idols: my desire for perfect health, my belief that money could keep us happy forever, and my attachment to our comfortable home. When I was fired, the first thing I did was pray: "Help me be wise to tell my wife. Help me accompany her well. Help me stay responsible for my family. I trust you. Lead me." She was very sad, but we prayed together and found courage.

In this same season, we lost family members and saw family separate. My wife's job was also at risk, and she had to be away for two weeks.

What God taught me in the storm

I joined the worship team, to sing for the Lord's glory with my brothers in Christ. Like the opera singer in Ischia, I now sing for Him. It gave me a strong sense of God's love, more like a Father who loves me better than anyone, and showed me how to love Him back.

We started a Bible study group with the premarital couple, who are now our friends. The same people who once doubted me now walk with me. The group opened a way for me, a bad reader, to read the Bible and enjoy it. I learned that the Bible is a love story from God to us: it shows who we are, who He is, what He has done for us, and what we are called to do in His name, all for His glory.

Matthew 6 transformed how I see things. It warns against storing treasures on earth. I had just lost my earthly treasures: health, income and my comfortable home. This confirmed that God is my main and most wanted treasure. And even after losing all these things, I have seen God's provision. Nothing has been missing for me. In these troubled times, God's provision has kept us well fed and well accompanied. A few months ago, I couldn't sleep, thinking about how to invest my money or how to make more. Now, with no job, no home and other problems, I sleep well, trusting God, who feeds the birds of the air and sustains everything (Matthew 6:26).

The time alone while my wife was away showed me another mistake: I had been using her as a bridge to God. My relationship with God must be direct. Jesus Christ is the only mediator, and I must not put anyone between God and me.

Testifying about the light

I am not trained to be a preacher. What I do is share my testimony. I have seen it change people's perspective and bring hope to people going through hard times. On my trip, I found John the Baptist's purpose beautiful: to come and testify about the light. Today, that is what I try to do. I hope this brings others closer to God.

If this testimony has touched you, I ask you to pray for me: that God keeps showing me His glory, that He keeps leading me, and that I reflect His glory in everything I do.

This is my testimony.
